Visual Neuroscience & Consciousness

Rapid Alternation of Conscious & Suppressed Stimulus Representations in the Primate Brain

Key findings, population dynamics, and implications from Doris Tsao's lab preprint.

Core Insight: Rather than suppressing unseen stimuli into noise, neural populations in inferotemporal and prefrontal cortex rapidly alternate between conscious and suppressed face representations ~15 times per second (~60 ms dwell time).

Janis Hesse, S. Lanfranchi, C. McGrory, Y. Shi, T. A. Engel, and Doris Y. Tsao
UC Berkeley, Caltech BBE, Princeton, HHMI

bioRxiv preprint (Oct 16, 2026) • DOI: 10.64898/2026.10.15.759299 F01, F02, F03, F05, F06
Background & Puzzle

The 30-Year Mystery: Where is Consciousness in the Cortex?

For over three decades, neuroscientists used binocular rivalry to dissociate constant physical sensory input from alternating conscious perception.

Classical Expectation

High-level visual areas (such as the inferotemporal cortex, IT) should strongly fire for conscious stimuli and shut down for suppressed ones.

The Empirical Puzzle

When averaged over hundreds of milliseconds, the observed modulation was surprisingly weak — showing an average slope of only 0.23.

Did the brain discard the suppressed image, or was something fundamental obscured by time averaging?

Time-averaged modulation slope ≈ 0.23 F09, F27
Experimental Paradigm

Binocular Flash Suppression (BFS)

The authors employed binocular flash suppression (a robust variant of binocular rivalry):

  • Priming stimulus: One eye views a face stimulus for 1 or 2 seconds.
  • Flash stimulus: A second face is abruptly flashed to the contralateral eye.
  • Perceptual outcome: The flashed image overwhelmingly wins conscious perception (validated in 14 humans at 82% dominance).
Stimuli subtended 7° visual angle using red-cyan stereoscopic anaglyph glasses, allowing deterministic control over stimulus timing without motor report confounds.
Human behavioral validation: 82% flash dominance F09, F11, F12, F13, F14, F25
Electrophysiology

Massive Neuropixels Recording Across 4 Face Patches

High-density recordings were conducted in two male rhesus macaques using Neuropixels 1.0 NHP probes:

Inferotemporal (IT) Cortex

Patch ML (middle lateral, face feature encoding)
Patch AM (anterior medial, viewpoint-invariant identity)

Prefrontal Cortex (PFC)

Patch PO (orbital prefrontal patch)
Patch PVL (ventrolateral prefrontal patch)

A total of 3,501 visual responsive neurons were recorded across sessions, with 661 prefrontal neurons analyzed simultaneously.

2 macaques • 3,501 visual units • 4 patches F15, F16, F17, F18, F19
Analytical Framework

No-Report Paradigm & 10 ms Hidden Markov Models

To eliminate motor report confounds, monkeys did not press buttons. Instead, conscious and suppressed states were inferred from single-trial population spiking:

  • Spikes were binned in 10 ms sliding time windows (analysis bin, not state duration).
  • A Poisson Log-Likelihood Ratio (LLR) matched population patterns against unconfounded templates.
  • A 2-state Hidden Markov Model (HMM) decoded transitions between states.
The 2-state switching model outperformed single-state static models in 19 out of 19 sessions.
10 ms Poisson LLR + 2-state HMM • 19/19 sessions win F23, F29, F38
Primary Finding

Populations Rapidly Alternate at ~15 Hz

Within individual trials, population representations did not remain steady:

State 1: Conscious Representation

Represents the perceived face. 10-class single-trial decoding accuracy exceeds 61% (chance is 10%).

State 2: Suppressed Representation

Represents the invisible face. Decodes suppressed face features with high fidelity.

Oscillation Frequency: Populations alternate between State 1 and State 2 ~15 times per second (~15 Hz), with an average dwell time of ~60 ms (mean 67 ms, median 46–64 ms).
Alternation ≈ 15 Hz • Mean dwell time ≈ 67 ms F28, F30, F31, F36, F67
Mechanism

The Unseen Face Occupies the Full Face Space

Crucially, the suppressed stimulus is not an attenuated or noisy phantom:

  • Full Geometry: During State 2 epochs, the suppressed face occupies the exact same 50-dimensional feature "face space" as when seen alone.
  • Firing Rates: Overall firing rate in State 2 is actually slightly higher than in State 1.
  • Single Population: The exact same neurons represent both faces in turn; there are not two separate populations.
High-level visual cortex maintains rich, full-fidelity representations of sensory stimuli even when completely suppressed from subjective awareness.
Same neurons switch content • Suppressed stimulus fully represented F41, F42, F53
Quantitative Findings

Effect Size: 1.21 Tuning Gain Ratio

What distinguishes the conscious representation from the suppressed one at the single-neuron level?

Tuning Gain: Neurons exhibit a 1.21 gain ratio during conscious state micro-epochs relative to suppressed state micro-epochs (gain correlation r = 0.90, comparison p = 3 × 10−40).

Important Scientific Distinction: The scientific effect size is the 1.21 ratio (a 21% gain increase), not the extreme p-value (which is a consequence of sample size and gain comparison context).

Effect size: 1.21 gain ratio (r = 0.90, p = 3x10^-40) F39, F40
Theoretical Resolution

How Time-Averaging Created the Illusion of Weak Modulation

This 15 Hz multiplexing explains why past studies failed to find strong signals in IT cortex:

Single-Trial Reality

Strong, opposing representations alternate every ~60 ms. When State 1 is active, conscious gain is +21%.

Time-Averaged Artifact

Averaging over 500–700 ms blends the positive and negative epochs together, yielding a flat slope of 0.23.

When micro-epoch gain is equalized, residual correlation vanishes (r = −0.02), confirming that cancellation accounts for the apparent weak modulation in this dataset.
Time-averaging cancellation accounts for weak modulation F27, F43
Cortical Scope

Widespread Phenomena: Qualitatively Similar in PFC

Does rapid alternation occur exclusively in visual sensory cortex?

  • Prefrontal Cortex: Both patch PO and PVL in the prefrontal cortex also exhibit qualitatively similar rapid alternation.
  • Coordination: Switching coordination between IT patches (ML and AM) is weak, indicating local micro-epoch dynamics.
  • Reconstruction: PFC reconstruction fidelity is lower (priming EV < 80%, shape features < 30%), reflecting its abstract encoding.
The authors suggest that temporal multiplexing may be a general, widespread cortical mechanism for information handling.
PFC shows qualitatively similar alternation • Authors: likely widespread F47, F48, F50, F52
Critical Guardrails

What This Research Does NOT Mean

✗ "Consciousness Has a Frame Rate"

Permanently Contradicted: Doris Tsao explicitly clarified: "We did not prove consciousness has a frame rate." Neural representation alternation does not imply subjective experience is broken into discrete frames.

! Theoretical Attribution Guardrails

The preprint does not test or cite GNWT or IIT. Connecting this 15 Hz multiplexing to Global Workspace ignition or Integrated Information is external interpretation.

Frequency Specificity: 15 Hz is in the low-beta range, not theta (4–8 Hz) or alpha (8–12 Hz).
Tsao clarification (preliminary) • No frame rate claim F55, F56, F57
Summary & Horizons

Key Takeaways & Open Scientific Questions

  • Sensory Richness: The brain does not suppress unseen visual information; it maintains it within a rapid temporal multiplex.
  • 15 Hz Multiplexing: Conscious and suppressed representations trade places ~15 times per second (~60 ms dwell time).
  • The Readout Enigma: Why do only certain micro-epochs break into subjective perception while others remain subconscious?
